02Our approach

How we'll help Ryan, in four steps

Step 1 of 4 · Nov–Dec

First, we look for the rat

Think of Ryan's brain as the attic of a house, wired to every room: words, movement, calm. When something is inflaming his gut and body, it's like a rat up there chewing the wires.

Therapy can run new wiring, but if the rat is still in the attic, it chews that back up too. So before anything else, we find out what the rat is.

How we look
Gut ZoomerStool test: gut lining, gut bacteria, signs of infection
Organic AcidsUrine test: yeast and bacteria byproducts, energy, brain chemistry

Both collected at home. Ryan has never had labs, so this is the first real look.

Step 2 of 4 · From Dec 18

Then we kill the rat

Once his labs are back, Dr. Jared builds the plan to clear out whatever they find, so the brain feels safe enough to rewire.

Level 6Cortical function
Communication
What it is

The thinking brain: language, understanding and social skills. This is where words show up.

What we see in Ryan

No spoken words yet, but he understands more than he can say. He copies sounds from shows he loves, copies signs like “cookie” when you show him first, points, leads you by the hand, and has a strong memory for things he has seen.

At home

Sounds, signs and first words through play and repetition, built on the levels below and alongside his speech therapy.

Level 5Autonomic & cranial
Safety and calmCommunication
What it is

The nerves that run the face, mouth, voice and breathing, plus the body's calm-or-alert switch (including the vagus nerve).

What we see in Ryan

He breathes through his mouth and rarely sneezes. Loud clapping or yelling makes him cover his ears and get upset. He moans and hums as he moves around.

At home

Calming work for the vagus nerve, mouth and breath activities that lead toward sounds, and slowly building his comfort with noise.

Level 4Oculomotor control
Communication
What it is

How the eyes move, track and focus together. It feeds attention, learning and eye contact.

What we see in Ryan

Eye contact dropped around 6 months and is still limited. He watches small, moving things like ants and bugs very closely.

At home

Eye tracking and focusing games, using the glasses in his home kit.

Level 3Vestibular integration
Safety and calmMotor skills
What it is

The inner-ear sense of movement and balance. It tells the brain where the body is and helps it feel safe moving.

What we see in Ryan

He loves the trampoline, the vibration pad and dancing. That drive to move is something we put to work in his sessions.

At home

Structured trampoline, vibration and movement games as part of each session, using the gear you already have.

Level 2Postural stability
Motor skills
What it is

The core strength and balance to stand and walk steadily, so the brain isn't spending energy just holding the body up.

What we see in Ryan

Your top concern. He walks on his toes some of the time and his feet turn in or out. Balance and clumsiness were rated low concern on his intake.

At home

Core, balance and walking work, done in short, fun blocks.

Level 1Tone, symmetry & reflexes
Motor skills
What it is

Muscle tone, left-right balance in the body, and baby (primitive) reflexes that should fade as children grow.

What we see in Ryan

He skipped crawling and walked early, and sometimes his hands curl when he grips things. Dr. Kyle's team checks his primitive reflexes at his first assessment.

At home

Crawling patterns, reflex movements and hand work, matched to what his assessment shows.

Level 0Foundation: gut, immune & regulation
Gut and dietSafety and calm
What it is

The body's base: gut health, the immune system and how settled his nervous system is. If this level is struggling, everything above it has less to build on.

What we see in Ryan

He leans toward carbs and salty foods like pizza and chips, but he's opening up to blueberries and chicken. He sleeps well. He has never had labs.

At home

His Gut Zoomer and Organic Acids labs, Dr. Jared's supplement and diet plan, moving away from carbs step by step, and light therapy from his home kit.

Based on your intake and our calls, not a diagnosis. Dr. Kyle's team confirms each level at Ryan's first assessment.

Why labs first

Test, then build his plan

You told us you see Ryan's challenges as neurological, not just behavioral, and that the leucovorin he was prescribed doesn't feel like enough. Rather than guess at supplements, we find the rat in the attic first, testing his gut and checking for signs of infection. The results show Dr. Kyle's team where the foundation work starts and give Dr. Jared, who leads our work on the immune side and infections, what he needs to build Ryan's supplement and diet plan.

Gut ZoomerOrganic AcidsResults review with Dr. Jared

What the two labs look at

Gut Zoomer · stool

His gut lining, including leaky-gut markers; the balance and variety of his gut bacteria; signs of infection from bacteria, yeast or parasites; inflammation; and how well he digests and absorbs food.

Organic Acids · urine

Byproducts of yeast and bacteria in his gut, how his body makes energy, and markers tied to brain chemistry and nutrients.
